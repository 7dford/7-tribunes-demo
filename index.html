7Tribunes Method

How 7Tribunes builds a Brief

1. Establish the fact spine. Identify confirmed, reported, disputed, unknown and changed information using multiple independent sources.

2. Score reporting quality separately. Reliability considers sourcing, evidence, headline accuracy, transparency, corrections and reporting-versus-opinion separation.

3. Analyze perspective. Framing analysis considers language, emphasis, quoted authorities, contextual differences and ideological positioning. Ratings include confidence.

4. Select credible comparisons. The app never chooses a low-quality article merely to create left/right symmetry.

5. Explain the result. Users can see why a story or article was selected and where uncertainty remains.

What drives the Impact Score?

7Tribunes editorial significance signal · 0–100

The Impact Score helps 7Tribunes decide which stories deserve the greatest prominence. It measures potential public consequence—not popularity, ideology, or whether a story is favorable to one side.

  • Public consequence: How significantly could the event affect people, institutions, rights, services, or major decisions?
  • Breadth of impact: How many people, communities, sectors, or geographic areas may be affected?
  • Geographic relevance: How directly does the story matter to the audience and the category in which it appears?
  • Public safety and household/economic effect: Does it affect health, safety, essential services, finances, jobs, costs, or daily life?
  • Official action and magnitude: Does it involve consequential government, court, institutional, emergency, or other authoritative action?
  • Source strength and coverage breadth: Is the event supported by credible reporting and, when available, primary evidence or multiple independent sources?
  • Recency and persistence: Is the development new, still unfolding, or likely to remain consequential beyond a short news cycle?

What does not drive the score: partisan viewpoint, advertiser interest, clicks, outrage, or raw social-media reaction totals. Social Heat can help identify stories worth investigating, but it does not substitute for verified impact.

The Impact Score is an editorial ranking aid. It is not a truth score, political-bias rating, or prediction. Top 7 freshness rule: a story may hold a Top 7 slot for no more than 24 hours. A materially new development is treated as a new story update and must be re-ranked. The methodology will be refined and documented as 7Tribunes develops.
